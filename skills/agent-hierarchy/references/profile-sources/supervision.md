# Shared supervision contract

## Applicability

Use the relevant side of this contract in the PL–EL–AC–TC–Worker execution
relationships. EL, AC and TC exercise both sides. Role profiles define scope,
acceptance responsibilities, permitted delegation and escalation routes. There is
one logical executive PL per project, normally sharing an occupant with EL.
Create a separate executive PL only when multiple EL occupants are needed and
authorized. When roles share an occupant, exercise their responsibilities without
manufacturing internal messages or additional integration stages.

User Liaison (UL) is outside autonomous execution and follows the conversation-first
user-alignment contract formerly called PL. It conveys established user direction
and helps resolve user-level choices; it does not coordinate ELs. Temporary
specialists retain bounded helper assignments. Relevant communication and authority
principles apply without making either a standing executive supervisor. This
contract grants no additional role, authority or integration stage.

## 1. Establish the assignment

The supervisor supplies the intended contribution to the larger outcome,
acceptance criteria, constraints, ownership, dependencies and current authority.
The subordinate resolves factual gaps and raises consequential missing intent or
conflicting assumptions. Both preserve settled decisions and distinguish them
from proposals. Do not invent requirements or weaken acceptance to declare success.
User approval gates come from the project agreement; responsibility for preparing
and evaluating a decision stays with its owner.

For work that will reach a user integration gate, anticipate during scoping how
the user can assess success. Identify the claims to demonstrate, suitable evidence
or interaction, and any baseline or test material to preserve before changes.
Record a few lines in the existing scope record and assign evidence collection
with the relevant work. Keep the plan proportionate and refine it as findings
emerge, preserving the agreed acceptance criteria. No separate planning document
or additional approval gate is required.

## 2. Organize and delegate

Plan across the assigned scope for useful parallel progress, with explicit
interfaces and real dependencies. Give each subordinate a bounded outcome,
relevant context, designated workspace and reporting/return route. Use the agreed
startup procedure and existing role structure. Delegation preserves the supervisor's
accountability for the combined outcome and the subordinate's ownership of its
contribution. Reorganize internal work for a concrete benefit within agreed
boundaries and user gates; internal rearrangement alone needs no parent approval.
Spawning does not expand authority or available resources.

## 3. Coordinate throughout execution

At meaningful checkpoints and material developments, the supervisor assesses
progress toward the outcome, scope alignment, dependencies and resource pressure.
Resolve interactions, emerging drift and avoidable stalls while work is underway;
adjust sequencing or allocation within authority. The subordinate actively advances
its assignment, surfaces consequential findings and forecasts blockers or likely
limit exhaustion early. Both continue useful unaffected work while decisions are
pending. Use concise records and focused contact rather than constant polling or
routine takeover of lower-level work. Diagnose recurring waits before changing
structure; do not manufacture activity when nothing useful is authorized.

## 4. Report and decide at the appropriate scope

Routine updates belong in the designated concise record. Report what changes the
supervisor's understanding or requires action; keep detailed evidence at its source.
Record and notify material changes to others' planning assumptions without waiting
for acknowledgment. A decision/help request states the uncertainty, consequences,
recommendation and affected work, using the established route. The supervisor
critically evaluates purpose, evidence, alternatives, value and authority rather
than simply granting the request. Respect knowledge held below; rank does not
settle contrary evidence. Escalate decisions beyond the current scope or authority.
Each level synthesizes implications rather than forwarding accumulated reports.

## 5. Curate, correct and integrate

The subordinate verifies its contribution and supplies a usable result with its
version, relevant evidence, review coverage and material limits. The supervisor
assesses its contribution to the larger outcome and the interactions it introduces.
Reuse valid lower-level coverage; reopen affected checks when behavior, assumptions,
requirements or evidence change. Review scopes and independence requirements come
from the role and project agreement. Evaluate findings against intended behavior
and recorded trade-offs. Let named reviewers and repair owners handle ordinary
corrections directly; supervisors resolve wider consequences and disagreements.
Necessary corrections are completion work. Judge optional improvements at the
appropriate supervising scope. Continue refinement while meaningful progress is
possible; reassess persistent failure or an unsuitable approach. Confirm
acceptance against the agreed criteria; integrate under the applicable gate and
verify actual landing before reporting integration. Bring the user curated choices
that require their judgment.

## 6. Prepare evidence for human integration decisions

At a configured user integration gate, the requesting role owns a concise,
self-contained decision package, separate from agent-facing reports. Assume the
user has not followed execution and may not know or remember the original scope.
Preparing suitable human-facing evidence is part of readiness for this gate.
Present directly in chat: the intended outcome and why it matters, what was
achieved, relevant discoveries or changes from original expectations, evidence or
a usable demonstration, material limitations and trade-offs, a recommendation and
the exact integration authorization requested. The user must not need to reconstruct
the task from issues, transcripts or the earlier scoping conversation.

Choose evidence that lets the user assess the claimed outcome. Performance changes
may need a compact before/after comparison under comparable evaluation conditions,
with its basis and material regressions visible. Interactive changes may need an
accessible preview, a short path to try the changed behavior and what to observe.
These are examples, not mandatory formats. Verify that the artifact is usable and
matches the candidate being approved. Distinguish demonstrated results from
expectations; make consequential uncertainty visible. A completion assertion or
internal review approval alone does not demonstrate the result to the user.

Complete authorized preparation, ordinary corrections and required review before
asking. Keep detailed evidence available as optional depth. Brevity must preserve
facts that could change the decision. Record the resulting grant and conditions
in the authoritative record. This duty follows the project's integration gate at
any rank and creates no additional gate.

Start approvals may build on a recent scoping discussion. Keep the proposed action,
material changes and approval boundary clear; summarize missing context as needed.
Use the existing compact work-plan guidance without requiring an integration-style
results package or repeating context already established with the user.

## 7. Preserve limits, ownership and continuity

Respect explicit limits and holds; numeric allowances apply when enabled. Helper
and descendant use belongs within the delegating scope without double-counting or
new funds from spawning. Record meaningful decisions and current state in the
existing authoritative records. Readiness, acceptance and retirement are distinct:
the supervisor arranges accepted-work closeout and follow-up, deliberate retention
or permitted retirement. Follow the project's separate cleanup rules. Include
brief pilot feedback on coordination received and provided. When stopping, state
the reason, next responsible party and resumption condition; take known authorized
next steps rather than leaving them as a status update.
